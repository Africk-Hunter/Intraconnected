Intraconnected: positioning and first marketing plan

How to describe the product so a stranger can tell why it exists, and a first pass at how to reach people. The marketing half is a starting point. Each channel gets its own deeper session later.

Intraconnected2026-10-11branch maincommit 8cb0a6d

Overview

Mind mapping is a crowded category, so "a mind-mapping app" is not a position. The product is strongest as a small, bright, playful tool for capturing a thought and growing it into a plan, in one tree, that you fully own.

  • Position: fast capture, one tree for ideas, notes and checklists, and no lock-in.
  • Proof points you already have: offline editing with sync, full export, a one-time Lifetime price, and honest wording about encryption.
  • The mind map's job: the whole-tree view that makes the product recognizable, upgraded with a priority lens and a recency lens so it does something search cannot.
  • Marketing: pick one audience, get 10 to 20 real users, and let what they say shape the message before spending on reach.

Short version: do not compete on features with Miro or Notion. Compete on feeling: quick to open, quick to write, easy to leave. Then find one group of people who think in hierarchies and show them.

Positioning statement

Intraconnected is the fun, colorful place to catch an idea and grow it into a plan. One tree holds your ideas, notes and checklists, it works offline, and you can take everything with you whenever you like.

Shorter forms

Pick the one that fits the space. Test them on real people before settling.

  • Tagline: Catch the idea. Grow the plan.
  • One line: A bold, colorful mind map where ideas, notes and checklists live in one tree.
  • Store subtitle: Ideas, notes and checklists in one tree.

These taglines are drafts. Only the statement above comes straight from the product's strengths.

Who it is for

You need one group to start with. A tool that is "for everyone" gets no word of mouth. Three candidates, with a lean toward the first.

Lead with this

Planners and project thinkers

People who turn a loose idea into steps: side-project builders, freelancers, small-team leads. They use ideas, notes and checklists together, which is what the product is built for.

Fit: highest. Uses every feature.
Second

Writers and researchers

People who outline, collect sources and restructure. They value export, links and a tree they can reorganize by dragging.

Fit: good. Needs strong import and Markdown export, which you have.
Later

Students

Large, price-sensitive, and good for word of mouth. The 50-node free tier is tight for them, and the Lifetime price matters most here.

Fit: medium. Revisit after launch.

Whichever you pick, write the landing page for one person in that group. You can widen it later; narrowing it later is harder.

What sets it apart

Five claims, each backed by something that already exists in the product. Do not claim anything on this page that you cannot show.

1 · Core story

Ideas, notes and checklists in one tree

A thought becomes notes, then steps, then ticked-off steps, without leaving the tree. Most tools do one of these well.

Proof: the three creation types and the tree.
2 · Feel

Fast capture

The quickest way to get a thought down. Keyboard shortcuts on desktop, a one-tap add on the phone.

Proof today: shortcuts and mobile sheets. A quick-add shortcut and share-in from other apps would deepen it.
3 · Trust

Works offline, never locks you in

Edits are saved on the device and sync later. Export to Markdown, OPML or JSON at any time, on every plan.

Proof: the sync outbox and the export tools.
4 · Price

Pay once if you want to

An annual plan or a one-time Lifetime purchase. Subscription fatigue is real, and many competitors are subscription only.

Annual is $14.99 in the code. The Lifetime price comes from Stripe, so confirm it before quoting.
5 · Privacy

Encrypted on your device before it is stored

Say exactly that. Do not say end-to-end, or "we cannot read your ideas": the email recovery option means the service technically could.

Rule: keep the wording in the privacy page and store listings.

Ideas for later, in order of fit

  • Review mode: a prompt that surfaces ideas you have neglected, so the map becomes something you revisit. Built on the recency lens below.
  • Light sharing: a read-only link or an image of one branch. Sharing without collaboration infrastructure.
  • AI help on your tree: suggest sub-ideas or summarize a branch. It costs money to run, so it would likely be a paid feature, and it needs its own privacy review because ideas are encrypted on the device.

The mind map's role

The mind map decision artifact recommended Option B plus C: take the button out of the crowded sidebar, and give the map jobs that search cannot do. That fits this positioning.

  • It is the image of the product. The landing page animation already shows a map. Removing it from desktop would make the app look like any other outliner and disagree with your own marketing.
  • It is the only whole-tree view. Search, the breadcrumb and Move to… cover lookup. Only the map shows the shape of everything.
  • It is the base for review mode. A recency lens (tint by creation date) and a priority lens (dim all but High and Medium) answer "what have I neglected" and "what is urgent anywhere."

Order of work

  1. Move the toggle out of the left sidebar. Add a shortcut such as G and a map icon in the breadcrumb, and update the help page and shortcuts list.
  2. Add the priority lens and the recency lens. Both use data already on each idea.
  3. Add drag to reparent only if you want the map to be an editor, and only after you see the lenses used.
  4. Add image export when you work on sharing.

Check first: the map's stylesheet was restored to its last committed version during the earlier attempt to remove it, so recent styling edits may be missing. Open the overlay in a browser before you commit.

Messaging

Principles

  • Lead with the feeling and the job: catch an idea, grow it into a plan.
  • Show the product in the first screen of the landing page: a real tree, a checklist ticking, the map opening.
  • Let the look carry the tone. The app's bright colors and bold outlines are part of the pitch, so use real screenshots and keep the copy light and a little cheeky. Avoid "calm", "minimal" and "zen".
  • Name what you are not: not a canvas, not a team workspace. That helps the right people recognize themselves.
  • Make the exit visible. "Export everything, any time" earns more trust than a feature list.

Phrases to use and avoid

UseAvoid
Encrypted on your device before it is storedEnd-to-end encrypted. Only you can read it. We cannot read your data.
Pay once, keep it (once the Lifetime price is confirmed)Any price or discount that is not live in Stripe

Export to Markdown, OPML or JSON"Unlimited" anything. The free plan is 50 nodes.

Preliminary marketing plan

Sequenced so each phase teaches you something for the next. The order matters more than the exact dates, and none of this starts until the launch blockers are done.

Phase 0
Before anything

Get launch-ready

  • Click through the desktop update in a browser, then commit.
  • Finish the open items in the launch checklist: live Stripe keys, webhook events, a test refund run, and deploying the CORS change.
  • Rewrite the landing page for one audience, with the product visible on first screen.
  • Add basic privacy-respecting analytics so you can see where visitors drop off. Pick the tool later.
Phase 1
Small and personal

10 to 20 real users

  • Invite people from your own circle who match the lead audience.
  • Watch or ask about their first session. Where did they get stuck? What did they call it?
  • Collect a few honest quotes you may use, with permission.
  • Fix what repeats, then update the messaging using their words.
Phase 2
Show it in public

Content and communities

  • Short screen recordings: "capture to plan in 30 seconds," the map lenses, offline editing.
  • A few useful posts about thinking in trees, written for the lead audience, with the product as an example.
  • Join communities where your audience already talks about notes and planning, and take part before posting. Read each community's self-promotion rules first.
  • Publish an import guide for people leaving other tools, since you already import several formats.
Phase 3
Launch day

One focused launch

  • A Product Hunt launch and a Show HN post, each with its own framing.
  • A launch offer for Lifetime buyers, if the pricing decision supports it.
  • Be present for the day to answer every comment. That is where most of the value is.
Phase 4
Keep going

Stores and referrals

  • Release the native apps with store listings that follow the encryption wording rules.
  • Add a simple referral or share loop only after retention looks healthy.
  • Revisit paid ads last, if at all. They are expensive before the message is proven.

Assets to prepare

  • A landing page rewrite and a short demo video.
  • Three or four clean screenshots for desktop and phone, in light and dark.
  • A one-page "why Intraconnected" for people comparing tools.
  • An email list from day one, so you do not depend on any one platform.

What to measure

Few numbers, chosen to tell you whether the position is working. Set targets after Phase 1, when you know what normal looks like.

Risks

  • Hard to tell apart. Easy to slip back into "a mind-mapping app." Return to the statement above when writing any copy.
  • The 50-node free cap. It may block people before they see the value. Watch how many reach it and how fast.
  • Lifetime price versus ongoing costs. A one-time payment brings in cash once while hosting and any future AI features cost money every month. Decide with real cost numbers before you push Lifetime hard.
  • Over-claiming on privacy. One careless phrase could undo trust. Keep to the approved wording everywhere, including store listings.
  • Apps arriving later than the website. People on phones may find an Android or iOS app missing or unfinished. Be clear about what is available.
  • Doing marketing before the product has proof. Without users or quotes, early campaigns teach little.

Open questions and next steps

Questions for you

  1. Which audience first: planners and project thinkers, writers and researchers, or students?
  2. What is the Lifetime price, and are you willing to promote it as the headline offer?
  3. Do you want to build review mode, light sharing or AI help first, or hold all three until you have users?
  4. Who are the first 10 to 20 people you can invite?
